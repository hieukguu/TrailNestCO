Overview

Why consider a rebounder?

A rebounder is a mini trampoline built for exercise rather than play. The appeal is fairly simple: the mat absorbs a large share of the force that a hard floor would otherwise send back through your ankles, knees and hips. That makes it a common choice for people who want cardio at home but have reasons to avoid running or high-impact classes.

It is also one of the few pieces of cardio equipment that genuinely disappears when you are done with it. A folded rebounder slides under a bed. A treadmill does not.

  • Cardio at home without a treadmill footprint or gym membership.
  • Low-impact movement that can be a joint-friendly alternative to some higher-impact exercises.
  • May support balance and coordination, since an unstable surface asks more of the stabilising muscles than a flat floor does.
  • Space-efficient — folds flat for storage between sessions.
  • Suits short daily routines. Cellercise's own beginner guidance suggests starting with a few minutes a day rather than long sessions.

Results vary depending on the user, the routine and how consistently it is followed. A rebounder is a tool, not an outcome.

Before you start

This page is a product comparison, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, are recovering from an injury, or have concerns about balance, speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new exercise programme.

Key Features

What sets the Cellerciser® apart

The details Cellercise builds its case on — and what each one means in practice.

1. TriDaptable® Spring System

The springs are tiered and tapered rather than uniform, which Cellercise says lets them stretch from the middle and absorb weight progressively. The practical claim is that the same unit adapts to different users instead of feeling stiff for a lighter person and bottoming out for a heavier one.

They are steel, not bungee cord — Cellercise argues this is why they do not stretch out and lose responsiveness the way elastic systems can.

2. Foldable Design

Both fold styles collapse the frame rather than requiring disassembly. The Bi-Fold folds in half to roughly 41 × 21 × 4 in. The Tri-Fold folds into thirds to roughly 22 × 24 × 6 in, which is the more compact of the two despite being the deeper package.

If you are choosing mainly on storage, that difference in shape matters more than the raw dimensions — a squarer folded unit fits a closet, a long flat one fits under a bed.

3. All-Steel Construction

Frame, hinges and springs are steel. Cellercise contrasts this with rebounders that use plastic components, and states its all-steel frame carries a lifetime warranty. The mat is a synthetic material the brand says resists stretching and erosion.

Steel adds weight — the units run roughly 33 to 46 lb depending on model and whether a bar is fitted — so the wheeled case on the Tri-Fold Pro is more useful than it first appears.

4. Balance Bar Options

Two of the three models ship with a bar: a streamlined bar on the Tri-Fold Pro and a removable bar on the Bi-Fold with Balance Bar. Cellercise notes the bar is intentionally not rigidly fixed, on the reasoning that a slightly mobile handhold encourages you to develop balance rather than lean on it.

A bar is worth considering if balance is a concern or if you are new to an unstable surface. It is not required to use the equipment.

Every model in the range folds and ships with a case. The Tri-Fold Pro's case adds a built-in dolly and wheels, which is the feature that separates "stores away" from "moves between rooms or gets taken with you".

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Answers below reflect information published by Cellercise. Specifications and terms can change — the product pages are authoritative.

What is a rebounder?
A rebounder is a mini trampoline built for exercise rather than play. Cellercise describes it as weight-bearing activity without the jar of typical exercise, which is why rebounders are often chosen as a lower-impact alternative to running or jumping on hard surfaces.
Which Cellerciser model is the most portable?
The Tri-Fold Pro. It folds into thirds and ships in a 22 × 24 × 6 in footprint, and Cellercise states the folded tri-fold fits many airplane overhead compartments. It also comes with a travel case that has a built-in dolly and wheels.
Which model includes a balance bar?
Two of them. The Tri-Fold Pro includes a streamlined balance bar, and the Bi-Fold Rebounder with Balance Bar includes a removable bar. The standard Bi-Fold Rebounder does not include one.
Does the Cellerciser fold for storage?
Yes. Every model in this comparison folds. The Bi-Fold folds in half, the Tri-Fold folds into thirds. Cellercise notes a folded unit can be stored under a bed or behind a door.
What is the maximum supported user weight?
Cellercise states the Cellerciser is warranted to support 400 pounds, and the Bi-Fold product page lists a 15 to 400 lb range. The Bi-Fold with Balance Bar page does not publish a figure, so confirm it on the product page before ordering.
What is included with each model?
The Tri-Fold Pro ships with a streamlined balance bar, a wheeled travel case, an instructional DVD with online video access, and an owner's manual. The Bi-Fold with Balance Bar ships with a removable balance bar, carrying case, instructional DVD with online access, and owner's manual. The standard Bi-Fold ships with a carrying case and support material. Promotional inclusions change — check the product page.
Does Cellerciser offer a warranty?
Coverage differs by model. The Tri-Fold Pro and the Bi-Fold with Balance Bar list a lifetime warranty on frame and springs. The standard Bi-Fold page lists a lifetime warranty on frame, rubber feet, bullhorns and pins, with 5 years on springs and mat. Check the product page for current terms.
Is rebounding suitable for beginners?
Cellercise publishes a four-week beginner progression and suggests starting with a few minutes a day rather than high jumping. Results vary by person. Anyone with a health condition, or recovering from an injury, should speak with a qualified healthcare professional before starting a new exercise programme.
Where can I check current pricing and availability?
On the Cellercise product pages. Prices, promotions and stock change, and Cellercise has listed some models as backordered in the past, so the product page is the only reliable source.
